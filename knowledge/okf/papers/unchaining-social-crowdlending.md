# Unchaining Social Businesses - Blockchain as the Basic Technology of a Crowdlending Platform

**Authors:** Andre Schweizer, Vincent Schlatt, Nils Urbach, Gilbert Fridgen  
**Venue:** ICIS 2017 Proceedings  
**Link:** https://aisel.aisnet.org/icis2017/TransformingSociety/Presentations/8

## Summary

Studying a crowdlending social business, the paper derives 17 objectives for a blockchain-based crowdlending prototype and evaluates the prototype against a non-blockchain solution, deriving generic knowledge on blockchain applications for social business.

## Artifact

A blockchain-based crowdlending platform prototype for social businesses.

## Methodology

Design science research; case study of a crowdlending social business; 17 solution objectives; prototype and evaluation vs. non-blockchain solution.

## DSR grid

The six core dimensions of this DSR project, following the [DSR grid](../about-the-dsr-grid.md) (vom Brocke & Maedche, 2019):

* **Problem description.** Social businesses face conflicting objectives (social purpose versus competitive advantage and financial sustainability), and crowdlending platforms need trustworthy, low-cost infrastructure.
* **Input knowledge.** The Peffers et al. DSR process; social-business and crowdlending literature; market-design parameters; blockchain and smart contracts.
* **Research process.** Design science research (Peffers et al.): a case study with a founder interview, a prototype, and evaluation against a non-blockchain solution.
* **Key concepts.** Blockchain, social business, smart contracts, crowdlending, design science.
* **Solution description.** A blockchain-based crowdlending platform prototype for social businesses. Solution-space representation: Instantiation (prototype) driven by seventeen design objectives (model / nascent design knowledge).
* **Output knowledge.** Seventeen objectives for the blockchain crowdlending prototype, plus generic knowledge on blockchain for social business.

The *output knowledge* of this project is captured as the atomic design-knowledge concepts listed below.

## Design knowledge

* [Design objective DO1: Financial sustainability](../design-knowledge/unchaining-social-crowdlending-do1.md) - Since the social business is not based on donations, it must support the recovery of costs (Yunus et al. 2010). Thus, a balanced profit equation between cost and revenue is needed (Boons and Lüdeke-Freund 2013). Further, the startup's management indicated that, owing to their long-term commitment to investors and students, financial sustainability is necessary.
* [Design objective DO2: Social purpose](../design-knowledge/unchaining-social-crowdlending-do2.md) - Social businesses act as a change agent for the world and pursue the creation of social benefits (Yunus et al. 2010). Thus, although the prototype is required to cover its costs, the social perspective and more cause-driven character are first priority (Boons and Lüdeke-Freund 2013).
* [Design objective DO3: Allow small amount investments](../design-knowledge/unchaining-social-crowdlending-do3.md) - The crowdlending platform needs to allow the investment of small financial amounts (Lins et al. 2016). Therefore, low transaction costs are necessary for achieving a cost-efficient social business investment model.
* [Design objective DO4: Provide editable information about funding projects](../design-knowledge/unchaining-social-crowdlending-do4.md) - The crowdlending platform needs to provide details about projects in search for funding, as information is seen as a crucial factor of the funding success (Lins et al. 2016; Overby et al. 2010). Furthermore, the ability to update the auction with recent project information has a positive effect on the funding outcome (Agrawal et al. 2014; Ward and Ramachandran 2010).
* [Design objective DO5: Provide mechanism to establish and measure user reputation](../design-knowledge/unchaining-social-crowdlending-do5.md) - To address the market design parameter of user reputation the platform needs to provide a feedback function, enabling that a user can provide insights about their experience with a specific user after a transaction (Cabral; McDonald and Slawson 2002).
* [Design objective DO6: Define and enforce platform rules](../design-knowledge/unchaining-social-crowdlending-do6.md) - The platform needs to be designed to act according to predefined rules (Agrawal et al. 2014). To adhere the existing standards, we follow industry regulation standards and define a maximum invest per investor as well as a maximum funding amount per student project.
* [Design objective DO7: Crowd due diligence](../design-knowledge/unchaining-social-crowdlending-do7.md) - Research studies on the crowdfunding platform Kickstarter suggest that a greater number of perspectives available to recognize something amiss are a useful tool to detect fraud (Agrawal et al. 2014). Thus, our platform must allow for comments to a project by any user.
* [Design objective DO8: Provision point mechanism](../design-knowledge/unchaining-social-crowdlending-do8.md) - The provision point mechanism is a common practice in crowdlending platforms to address the free-rider problem. Thus, our platform needs to include a parameter for minimum funding, which needs to be reached before funding is provided to a student (Agrawal et al. 2014).
* [Design objective DO9: Provide reporting functions](../design-knowledge/unchaining-social-crowdlending-do9.md) - For regularly reporting and statistics on the social business development, the prototype must provide the functionality to run reports on the transactions of a specific period.
* [Design objective DO10: Transaction time](../design-knowledge/unchaining-social-crowdlending-do10.md) - As soon as the funding limit is reached, the subsequent transactions should be executed for student projects to start.
* [Design objective DO11: Data persistency](../design-knowledge/unchaining-social-crowdlending-do11.md) - To ensure transparency, traceability, and archiving requirements, the prototype needs to store data persistently and immutably.
* [Design objective DO12: Transaction volume](../design-knowledge/unchaining-social-crowdlending-do12.md) - Currently, the volume of funded projects is fairly low; thus, the number of transactions should be manageable without constraints. However, an improved solution must be scalable.
* [Design objective DO13: Trust and personal identification](../design-knowledge/unchaining-social-crowdlending-do13.md) - Trust and personal identification mechanisms should be seamless and should not represent an entry barrier for potential users.
* [Design objective DO14: Reduction of manual activities](../design-knowledge/unchaining-social-crowdlending-do14.md) - The manual activities involved in the processing of the transactions and the management of project lifecycles should be further automated to reduce costs and the possibilities of fraud.
* [Design objective DO15: Reliable and trustworthy transaction processing](../design-knowledge/unchaining-social-crowdlending-do15.md) - To avoid malicious changes of funding related data, the prototype must be able to process transactions in a reliable and trusted way.
* [Design objective DO16: Stability of credit currency](../design-knowledge/unchaining-social-crowdlending-do16.md) - To ensure a stable and calculable payback amount, the credit currencies should not be subject to high fluctuations.
* [Design objective DO17: Avoidance of complex interfaces](../design-knowledge/unchaining-social-crowdlending-do17.md) - To reduce the implementation efforts and system maintenance, and to increase independence from legacy applications, the prototype must be developed in a way that avoids complex interfaces.
